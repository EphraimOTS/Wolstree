CBEX, PCEX and the Rise of "AI Trading" Ponzi Apps: How to Protect Your Money

By WolStreet Finance Desk · July 20, 2026 · 8 min read

Every year or two, a new "investment platform" goes viral in Nigeria — big returns, a slick app, an "AI" doing the trading for you. And every year or two, it eventually stops paying out, and the people who put money in are left with a login screen that won't load. We've seen the pattern with names like CBEX and PCEX. It will keep happening under new names unless people learn to spot it early.

How these schemes actually work

Almost none of them are really trading anything. Early users get paid — often on time, sometimes early — using money coming in from newer users, not from any actual investment return. That's the definition of a Ponzi scheme. The payouts you see from your friend or your cousin's screenshot aren't proof it works; they're the bait that gets the next round of deposits in.

The red flags — every single one applies here

  • Guaranteed or "fixed" daily/weekly returns. No legitimate trading strategy — human or AI — guarantees a fixed return. Markets don't work that way.
  • Returns that beat every legitimate asset class by a wide margin. If a "low risk" app claims to turn 5% a week, that's over 900% a month compounded — numbers no real fund on earth sustains.
  • Pressure to recruit others. If your "returns" improve by bringing in new investors, you are the product, not a customer.
  • Vague or unverifiable "AI" or "algorithm" claims. No audited track record, no regulator registration, no named, licensed fund manager.
  • Withdrawal friction. Delays, new "verification fees" to release your money, or sudden platform "maintenance" right when people try to cash out en masse.
Simple rule of thumb: If you can't explain — in one sentence, without saying "the app" — exactly what your money is invested in and how it earns a return, don't put money in.

If you've already put money in

  1. Stop depositing more immediately, even if you're told it will "unlock" your existing funds.
  2. Document everything — transaction receipts, screenshots, the app's registration claims (if any).
  3. Report to the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) and the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) Nigeria, which both handle unregistered investment scheme complaints.
  4. Warn people in your circle directly rather than staying quiet out of embarrassment — most victims of these schemes report finding out too late because no one close to them said anything.

Checking before you invest, anywhere

Nigeria's SEC maintains a public list of registered fund managers and investment platforms. Any legitimate platform managing pooled investor money should appear there, or be a licensed bank, broker, or fintech under CBN/SEC oversight. Two minutes of checking a register can save you a lot more than two minutes of regret.
