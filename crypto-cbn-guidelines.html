Crypto in Nigeria: What the CBN's Latest Guidelines Actually Mean for You

By WolStreet Finance Desk · June 29, 2026 · 6 min read

Crypto policy in Nigeria has had a bumpy few years — from an outright restriction on banks facilitating crypto transactions, to a reversal that opened the door to regulated engagement, to SEC frameworks for licensing exchanges. Here's how to think about where things stand, without treating any single headline as the final word.

The general direction of travel

After initially restricting banks from servicing crypto exchanges, Nigerian regulators shifted toward a licensing-based approach: allowing banks to open accounts for SEC-licensed Virtual Asset Service Providers (VASPs), while keeping unlicensed platforms outside the formal financial system. The intent is less about banning crypto and more about bringing exchanges, custody providers, and related businesses under the same anti-money-laundering and consumer protection expectations as other financial institutions.

What this means practically

  • Licensed platforms get easier banking access — meaning smoother naira on/off ramps for users of properly registered exchanges.
  • Unlicensed platforms remain higher-risk — not just regulatorily, but practically, since they sit outside the consumer protections and dispute processes licensed entities are expected to offer.
  • Taxation and disclosure expectations around crypto gains have tightened as authorities try to capture crypto activity within the tax net, similar to trends in many other markets.

How to protect yourself regardless of the headlines

  1. Prefer exchanges that are SEC-registered or clearly licensed in a recognized jurisdiction over ones with no public regulatory status.
  2. Keep records of your transactions — dates, amounts, and counter-value in Naira — both for your own risk tracking and in case tax reporting requirements apply to you.
  3. Treat any platform promising fixed, guaranteed crypto returns with the same skepticism as any other investment scheme — see our Scam Watch piece on exactly this pattern.
  4. Don't assume "crypto" and "the specific app you're using" carry the same risk profile — a well-established, licensed exchange and an obscure new app are not the same bet.
Regulation moves in real time: Rules around crypto in Nigeria have changed direction more than once in a few years. Check the CBN and SEC's official guidance directly before making decisions based on any single article, including this one.
